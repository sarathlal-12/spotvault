Why AirBridge

Your phone plays, your PC listens.

Wear your PC headphones and still listen to your phone. No cables, nothing to install on the phone.

Clear sound

Music plays cleanly, with no crackle, through whatever speakers or headphones your PC uses.

In sync with video

Instagram, YouTube and TikTok stay in sync. AirBridge keeps the delay to a tenth of a second.

Phone volume works

Your iPhone's volume buttons control the sound on the PC. Windows' volume sets the maximum.

Android too

Android phones connect over Bluetooth. AirBridge turns your PC into a Bluetooth speaker.

Stays out of the way

Lives in the system tray and can start with Windows, ready whenever your phone is.

Free and open

No account, no ads, nothing leaves your home network. The code is on GitHub.

Setup

Two minutes, once.

iPhone

Over Wi-Fi with AirPlay

  1. Install AirBridge on your PC. It appears in the system tray.
  2. Keep the phone and PC on the same Wi-Fi. In Windows, set that network to Private.
  3. Play something, open Control Center, tap the AirPlay icon on the music card and choose AirBridge.

Android

Over Bluetooth

  1. Right-click the AirBridge tray icon and choose Android / Bluetooth phones › Pair a new phone. Pair your phone with Windows.
  2. In the same menu, choose your phone › Let this phone play here.
  3. Play on the phone. Next time it connects by itself while AirBridge is running.

Windows says "Windows protected your PC"? AirBridge is a free app that isn't code-signed. Click More info › Run anyway.
iPhone doesn't list AirBridge? Check your Wi-Fi is set to Private in Windows and both are on the same network. Guest networks often block AirPlay.

Questions

Does it work with Screen Mirroring?

No, AirBridge receives audio only. Use the AirPlay button on the music card (or inside the app), not Screen Mirroring.

Is there a delay?

On iPhone about a tenth of a second, so video stays in sync. Bluetooth from Android has a little more delay, like most Bluetooth speakers.

Can I use my PC normally while it plays?

Yes. Your phone's audio mixes with the PC's own sounds through the same speakers or headphones.

Can an iPhone use Bluetooth instead?

Usually yes. Pair it with Windows and turn it on in the tray menu, the same as Android. AirPlay sounds better, though.

Is it safe? What does it send?

Audio goes straight from your phone to your PC on your own network. Nothing goes to the internet and there's no account.
